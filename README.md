# ANSELD — Systems-Test Prototype

A playable, low-fi graphical prototype of the ANSELD design: an unbodied intelligence
possessing hosts across a fifteen-year era of a salt-lake kingdom, aiming prophecies
and paying for rewinds in scars. **Code is the referee; the AI is only the storyteller.**

This is a testing instrument, not a product. Every open design decision from the
design document was resolved before this was built; the dials in the designer panel
are the *remaining* design work, by design.

## Running it

You need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) installed (it already is, if I built this on your machine).

Open a terminal in this folder and run:

```
npm install     (first time only)
npm run dev
```

Then open the address it prints (usually `http://localhost:5173`) in your browser.

## The two modes

- **Mock run** — Saltmere, a fixed scripted town. No AI, no cost, always identical.
  Use it to learn the interface and to verify the rules behave. The scripted
  storyteller understands a few phrases: *ferry / cross*, *study / ledger*,
  *read aloud / dock*, *confront the Warden*. Anything else passes time quietly.
- **Live run** — a fresh era generated by AI when you press the button, written
  turn by turn as you play. Needs your Anthropic API key (paste it on the start
  screen; it is stored only in your browser on this machine). A session costs
  pennies — the exact total is always visible in the top bar and itemized per
  call in the designer panel.

## How to play

- You are the unbodied intelligence. The **era grid** (right side) is the whole
  timeline: click any year of any body to enter it. Fresh windows are free.
- Re-entering a window you already occupied, or a body you have watched die,
  is a **rewind**: you are warned first, and it always costs **one scar**.
  What you rewind over becomes **unwitnessed time** — recorded forever.
  Knowledge you gained survives; nothing else does.
- **Scars are the only way to lose.** At the cap, Osric closes the count on you.
- **Prophecies** are sentences with blanks. **Aim** one to fill its blanks in
  your own words — once, irrevocably. If events brush an unaimed prophecy's
  subjects, it *stirs* (warning), then *locks carelessly* (decay) per its sealed
  sketch, written at its birth.
- The **prime prophecy's** three conditions are the win. They cannot be aimed —
  they are the door itself. A blind Clerk rules on fulfillment, literally,
  against hidden conditions fixed before play. You never see the hidden faces
  (unless you flip the designer toggle that reveals them).

## The designer panel (the point of all this)

Press **`** (backtick) or the ⚙ button. Everything is live:

- **Scar cap** — the loss line.
- **Tier 2 / Tier 3 thresholds** — when Osric localises, and when he predicts.
- **Osric intensity 0–3** — how physically present his attention becomes.
- **Contacts to decay** — how much brushing an unaimed prophecy survives.
- **Loose prophecy count** — applies to the next generated run.
- **Models** — which Claude model writes scenes, rules fulfillments, generates eras.
- **Reveal hidden faces** — designer eyes only; shows every hidden condition and
  sealed sketch in the prophecy panel.
- **AI call log** — every call, its tokens, its cost.

Tune the dials mid-run. The values that feel right *are* the remaining design decisions.

## A design consequence discovered in the build (flagged for playtesting)

Two locked rules collide in one place: *aiming is irrevocable* and *a rewind
un-happens everything since*. As built, **a rewind can un-happen an aim** —
the aim un-happens like any other act, and the scar is the price. This follows
Option C's own words ("the act un-happens; its consequences un-happen") and it
is priced, not free — but it does mean a scar buys back aimed words. Decide in
play whether that feels like a devil's bargain or a loophole; if it's a loophole,
the fix is one line in the referee (make `prophecy-aimed` a permanent event kind).

## For the record

- `npm test` — 26 automated checks of the rules: rewind pricing, the scar cap,
  decay tempo, aiming permanence, the fifty-year ceiling, sealed rooms, the
  Ledger's refusal to un-count.
- `node smoke.mjs` (with the dev server running) — drives a full mock run in a
  headless browser and screenshots every stage into `smoke-shots/`.
- `src/core/` is the machine: `types.ts` (vocabulary), `record.ts` (the Ledger),
  `referee.ts` (the rules). No AI imports anywhere in that folder — that is the
  entire point of the architecture.
